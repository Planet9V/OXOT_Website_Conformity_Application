An OT security assessment from OXOT starts where your risk actually lives: on the plant floor. We reason about process, safety and continuity first — not office IT copied onto operational technology. The goal is not another binder of findings, but a clear, prioritised picture of where you stand and what to do next, backed by evidence you own.

We work passive-first. Discovery is designed to run without disrupting production, and every conclusion traces back to something observable in your environment — not an assumption.

```keyfacts
Scope :: a site, a line or a defined zone — your choice
Duration :: typically 2–6 weeks depending on scope
Basis :: IEC 62443-3-2 risk assessment + your operational risk
Approach :: passive-first — no production impact
Output :: a prioritised risk register and a defensible roadmap
Deliverable :: decisions you can defend to a board or a regulator
```

## Why most OT security reviews miss the point

Most OT environments have been through some form of security review. Very few have a clear, prioritised picture of their actual operational risk — because most reviews arrive with IT assumptions, reach for a vulnerability scanner, and leave behind a list scored by CVSS. That list doesn't tell you what to fix first. It doesn't tell you what would stop the plant. And it aged the day it was printed.

```compare
The typical security review
- Starts from an IT scanner or a generic compliance checklist
- Rates findings by CVSS — a score built for IT, not operations
- A snapshot that ages before the report is filed
- A pile of findings handed over without a sequence or a rationale
- Consultant knowledge walks out when the engagement ends
---
An OXOT assessment
- Starts from your actual traffic, topology and process constraints
- Risk ranked by safety impact, continuity impact and production impact
- A structured model you own and can keep current
- A prioritised, sequenced roadmap — quick wins separated from structural fixes
- Understanding transferred to your own team throughout the work
```

## What you get

```cards
Asset & zone picture :: :: A clear inventory of assets, connections and zones — the map most OT environments cannot produce on demand and that every risk decision depends on.
Prioritised risk register :: :: Risks ranked by impact to safety, continuity and production. Not by CVSS severity and not alphabetically — by what matters to operations.
Quick wins vs. structural fixes :: :: A separated list: actions you can take in the next quarter, and the deeper investments that belong in a programme — so effort goes where it makes a difference immediately.
Architecture observations :: :: Where your current zone and conduit design leaves you exposed, and what practical changes would reduce that exposure without disrupting operations.
Regulatory gap summary :: :: Where you stand against the NIS2 risk-management measures and — where relevant — the Cyber Resilience Act product obligations and IEC 62443 target security levels.
Board-ready summary :: :: One page that leadership and auditors can act on. Not a 200-page PDF — a clear, honest picture of where you stand and what it will take to improve.
```

## How we deliver it

```timeline
1. Scope & safety review :: Agree the boundary, the crown jewels, and the rules of engagement. Safety is the first constraint — anything that could affect process continuity is agreed with operations before we start.
2. Passive discovery :: Observe network traffic, collect topology data and review available documentation without touching production systems. Passive data is supplemented by structured interviews with the engineers who actually run the plant — they know things no scanner will find.
3. Asset & zone mapping :: Build a structured picture of everything in scope: assets, firmware versions, communication paths, zone boundaries, conduits, and the data flows that cross trust boundaries. This becomes the foundation of a Cyber Digital Twin.
4. Risk analysis :: Map findings to your operational context using the IEC 62443-3-2 risk assessment methodology. Every finding is evaluated against three criteria — safety impact, continuity impact and production impact — not CVSS. The result is a risk register your operations team can challenge and own.
5. Findings workshop :: Walk your OT and IT security teams through what we found and how we rated it. Priority is agreed together — you understand your operational constraints better than we do, and the workshop is where that knowledge enters the picture.
6. Roadmap :: A sequenced set of actions: immediate steps you can take before the next maintenance window, and structural improvements staged over a realistic horizon. Every action traces back to a risk in the register.
```

## Framework alignment

| Framework | What this assessment addresses |
|---|---|
| **IEC 62443-3-2** | The risk assessment follows the 62443-3-2 methodology — target security levels per zone, threat and risk tolerance, and the gap between the security level achieved and the security level target |
| **NIS2 Art. 21(1)** | Article 21 requires "appropriate and proportionate technical and organisational measures" based on a risk analysis — the assessment provides the risk picture that makes "proportionate" defensible |
| **NIS2 Art. 21(2)(a)** | Policies on risk analysis and information system security — the assessment output is the evidence base for those policies |
| **NIS2 Art. 21(2)(d)** | Supply-chain security — remote access inventory and third-party connectivity exposure are part of the assessment scope |
| **Cyber Resilience Act** | For manufacturers in scope, the assessment identifies which OT assets and embedded software components carry CRA obligations and what their current exposure is |
| **EU AI Act** | Where AI systems interact with or control safety-relevant OT functions, the assessment maps their architectural position and the security controls around them |

## How it connects

An assessment is the start of everything else — you cannot prioritise what you cannot see, and you cannot defend what you cannot describe.

The asset and zone picture built during an assessment seeds the **[Cyber Digital Twin](/cyber-digital-twin)** — the living model that keeps risk decisions current as your environment evolves. The prioritised risk register is the natural input backlog for a **[security programme](/ot-security-programmes)**, and the security-level gap analysis is the starting point for defining your **[OT security baseline](/ot-security-baseline)**. Where the assessment finds segmentation gaps, it hands directly into **[Architecture & Segmentation](/architecture-segmentation)** design work. Where remote access is a key exposure — and it usually is — it scopes the **[Secure Remote Access](/secure-remote-access)** engagement.

In energy, water, logistics and manufacturing, the assessment is also the moment where your internal team begins to understand their own environment in a structured way. That understanding is the first step in **[Capability Transfer](/capability-transfer)**: you cannot own a security programme you cannot describe.
