The best OT security engagement ends with your team not needing us. Capability transfer is how OXOT makes that happen: we build the operating model, the roles, the runbooks and the confidence your internal team needs to run OT security themselves — and we do it by working alongside your people throughout the engagement, not by handing over a document at the end.

The outcome is owned capability, not a dependency on external expertise.

```keyfacts
Goal :: self-sufficiency — your team genuinely owns OT security
Format :: coaching, playbooks and working side by side — not a handover workshop
Audience :: OT engineering, IT security, operations and management
Artefacts :: operating model, RACI, runbooks, playbooks, training materials
Duration :: embedded across a programme — not a standalone phase at the end
Outcome :: capability your organisation keeps after we leave
```

## Why most OT security capability efforts fail

Two patterns recur consistently. The first is the knowledge handover at the end: consultants deliver a 200-page report, spend half a day walking through it, and leave. Nobody reads 200 pages. What gets retained is whatever the team already understood — the rest ages quietly in a shared drive.

The second is the consultant dependency trap. The engagement produces results, but every time the organisation needs to repeat anything — apply the baseline to a new site, respond to an alert, onboard a new vendor — they call the consultant. The operating cost of security remains external, and the internal capability never grows.

```compare
Knowledge that does not transfer
- Delivered as a document at the end of an engagement
- Expertise sits with the consultant, not with your team
- Processes that require specialist knowledge to operate
- No clear ownership: security happens when the consultant is on site
- Organisation calls the consultant every time the process repeats
---
Capability that takes root
- Built throughout the engagement — your people do the work alongside us
- Expertise is transferred by doing, not by reading
- Processes documented in runbooks your team helped write and has used
- Clear ownership: named roles with defined responsibilities and cadences
- Organisation runs the process itself — OXOT steps back deliberately
```

## What you get

```cards
OT security operating model :: :: A clear description of how OT security runs day to day in your organisation — the decisions that need to be made, by whom, on what cadence, and how OT, IT and operations interface on security matters. The operating model is the skeleton that holds everything else together.
Roles & RACI :: :: Clear ownership of every security activity: who decides, who does the work, who is consulted and who is informed. Not an aspirational org chart — a RACI built around the people who are actually available, with gaps acknowledged and a plan to close them.
Runbooks & playbooks :: :: Repeatable procedures for the things that recur: onboarding a new vendor, running the quarterly access review, responding to a network alert, applying the baseline to a new site, handling a patch that cannot be applied. Written by your team, with us, during the engagement — not by us and handed over.
Training & coaching :: :: Your team builds OT security skills by doing the work with us on real problems in your environment. The coaching is embedded in the delivery — not a course delivered in a conference room with synthetic examples.
Governance cadence :: :: A defined meeting structure and reporting rhythm that keeps OT security visible to management without requiring specialist knowledge to prepare. Board-level reporting, steering meetings, and operational reviews — all with templates your team can sustain.
Handover & review :: :: A deliberate, staged handover in which OXOT reduces its involvement across the programme while your team takes on more. Followed by a review after handover to confirm the capability is holding — and to address any gaps before they become problems.
```

## How we deliver it

```timeline
1. Capability assessment :: Begin by understanding honestly what your organisation currently has: the skills, the structure, the informal knowledge, and the gaps. This is not a gap analysis against an ideal — it is an honest picture of what you can build from. Setting the target operating model to match your actual starting point is what makes capability transfer realistic.
2. Target operating model :: Define how OT security should run in your organisation — not how it would run in an ideal world with unlimited resource, but how it will run given your people, your structure and your operational constraints. Roles, cadences, interfaces and escalation paths are agreed with the people who will fill them.
3. Embed and coach :: From the first day of delivery, your people are doing the work alongside us. We explain what we are doing and why. We ask them to do the next instance of each task while we support. We capture every process in a runbook as it happens — not after the fact. The goal is that by the end of the engagement, nothing we did is a mystery.
4. Document as you go :: Runbooks and playbooks are written during delivery, not assembled at the end. Each time a process is executed, we ask: could your team run this without us next time? If not, what needs to be captured? The documentation reflects what was actually done, not what was theoretically planned.
5. Progressive handover :: From the midpoint of the programme, OXOT deliberately steps back from activities your team is ready to own. We support, review and advise rather than lead. This is where capability transfer either works or it does not — the test is whether your team can do it without prompting.
6. Post-handover review :: After the formal engagement ends, OXOT returns for a structured review — typically 60–90 days later — to assess whether the capability is holding. We work through any processes that have stalled or drifted, address gaps, and confirm that the operating model is functioning as designed. The goal is a clean handoff, not an indefinite retainer.
```

| **NIS2 Art. 21(2)(g)** | Basic cyber hygiene practices and training — embedded coaching produces a team that understands OT security as part of their work, not as a compliance obligation managed elsewhere |

## How it connects

Capability transfer is not a standalone service — it is the layer that runs through every other OXOT engagement to make the results last.

An **[OT Security Assessment](/ot-security-assessments)** produces a risk register and a roadmap. Capability transfer ensures your team understands how to keep that risk register current, how to prioritise new findings, and how to run the next assessment themselves. The asset and zone inventory produced during the assessment becomes a living document your team maintains — not a snapshot that ages on a drive.

A **[security programme](/ot-security-programmes)** produces waves of improvements. Capability transfer is what stops the programme dying the moment external resource steps back. By the end of the programme, your internal team should be running the backlog, managing the governance cadence and measuring risk reduction without requiring OXOT to be present.

The **[OT Security Baseline](/ot-security-baseline)** gives your team a clear standard to maintain. Capability transfer produces the runbooks and evidence collection procedures that make the baseline an operational tool rather than a policy document — and the assurance cadence that confirms compliance is being maintained between audits.

**[Architecture & Segmentation](/architecture-segmentation)** and **[Secure Remote Access](/secure-remote-access)** both produce designs and rule-sets that need ongoing management. Capability transfer delivers the change management process, the conduit rule review cadence, and the access review procedure as runbooks your team can operate independently.

The **[Cyber Digital Twin](/cyber-digital-twin)** is what keeps all of this current as your estate evolves. Capability transfer includes training your team to update and interrogate the twin — so that when a new line goes in or a vendor relationship changes, the risk picture is updated by your people, not by a consultant you need to call.
