Most organisations don't lack findings — they lack a way to deliver on them. An OXOT security programme turns a pile of recommendations into a sequenced, governed, multi-site effort that produces measurable risk reduction, not just activity.

We prioritise by operational risk, work in waves you can absorb around your maintenance schedule, and report progress in terms your board and regulators understand. The result is a programme your own team increasingly owns — and eventually runs without us.

```keyfacts
Horizon :: typically 12–36 months, staged in waves
Scope :: across sites, business units or regions
Governance :: a steering model with clear KPIs and accountability
Basis :: risk-based prioritisation, IEC 62443 lifecycle
Model :: an owned Cyber Digital Twin keeps priorities current as the estate evolves
Outcome :: measurable, reportable risk reduction — not activity metrics
```

## Why most OT security efforts stall

The typical pattern: an assessment produces a findings report. The report lands on someone's desk. A few urgent items get fixed. The rest ages quietly while the threat landscape does not. A year later another assessment finds the same structural gaps, plus new ones. The organisation has spent money on security without achieving security.

```compare
Why programmes fail
- No governance — findings land with no owner and no deadline
- No sequencing — everything is "high priority" so nothing is
- Waves sized by the consultant, not by what operations can absorb
- Progress measured in tasks closed, not in risk reduced
- The programme stops when the budget runs out or the sponsor moves on
---
How an OXOT programme is different
- A steering model with defined roles, cadence and escalation paths
- A backlog prioritised by operational risk, sequenced into deliverable waves
- Wave sizing agreed with operations — around maintenance windows, not over them
- Progress tracked against the risk baseline — leadership sees risk reduced, not tickets closed
- Ownership transferred deliberately so the programme continues after we step back
```

## How we deliver it

```timeline
1. Mobilise :: Stand up the governance structure, confirm scope, agree the risk baseline and align the steering model with existing business governance. This is the phase most programmes skip and then regret — a programme without governance is a project list.
2. Prioritise :: Rank the full backlog by operational impact using the risk register from the assessment phase. Sequence it into waves that operations can absorb — typically three-month cycles aligned to existing maintenance schedules.
3. Execute wave one :: Deliver the first wave alongside your team. Quick wins build momentum and demonstrate that the programme is real. Each wave ends with a measurement against the risk baseline.
4. Measure & adjust :: After each wave, measure risk reduced, update the Cyber Digital Twin and re-prioritise the backlog. The threat landscape changes; so does your estate — the programme adapts.
5. Transfer ownership :: From wave two onwards, OXOT deliberately reduces its footprint and your team takes on more. By the final wave, your internal team is running the programme, supported by runbooks and an operating model they helped build.
6. Sustain :: The programme does not end when OXOT steps back. The Cyber Digital Twin, the governance model and the capability your team built keep it running — and keep your risk position improving.
```

## Framework alignment

| Framework | What this programme addresses |
|---|---|
| **IEC 62443-2-1** | The programme lifecycle — risk management, security programme requirements, policy and process — is the practical expression of the IEC 62443-2-1 CSMS lifecycle |
| **IEC 62443-3-3** | System security requirements and security levels are the technical specification behind each wave's remediation targets |
| **NIS2 Art. 20** | Article 20 requires management bodies to take responsibility for cybersecurity measures — the programme's governance model and board reporting are designed to make that accountability real |
| **NIS2 Art. 21** | The ten technical and organisational measures in Art. 21 form the structured framework for what each wave of the programme addresses |
| **NIS2 Art. 21(2)(f)** | Policies and procedures for assessing the effectiveness of risk-management measures — the programme's KPI structure and risk tracking are the direct answer to this requirement |
| **Cyber Resilience Act** | For manufacturers, the programme tracks product security obligations alongside OT risk — one programme, one model, not two parallel efforts |

## How it connects

A programme is the structured execution of what an **[assessment](/ot-security-assessments)** discovers. The assessment produces the risk register; the programme turns it into delivery. As the programme runs, the **[Cyber Digital Twin](/cyber-digital-twin)** keeps the risk picture current — without a living model, the backlog ages and becomes a list of yesterday's priorities.

The programme typically incorporates **[Architecture & Segmentation](/architecture-segmentation)** in the early waves, because segmentation reduces the blast radius of everything that follows. **[Secure Remote Access](/secure-remote-access)** often appears in wave one — remote access sprawl is one of the most common and most tractable entry points. The **[OT Security Baseline](/ot-security-baseline)** provides the floor that every site in the programme must reach by the end. And **[Capability Transfer](/capability-transfer)** is not a separate workstream — it runs through every wave, so that by the time the programme ends, the ownership is already inside your organisation.
