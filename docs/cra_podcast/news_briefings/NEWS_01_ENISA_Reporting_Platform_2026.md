Here is the top story impacting digital product manufacturers: the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity—ENISA—has confirmed that the Article 16 Single Reporting Platform will be fully operational ahead of September 11, 2026.

While the general CE marking obligations under the CRA take effect on December 11, 2027, Article 14 mandatory vulnerability and incident reporting becomes legally enforceable fifteen months earlier, on September 11, 2026.

Under this mandate, manufacturers of products with digital elements sold in the EU must submit an early warning notification within 24 hours of becoming aware of any actively exploited vulnerability or severe incident. This notification must be submitted directly through ENISA's centralized platform, which automatically routes the data to national Computer Security Incident Response Teams across all 27 EU member states.

The Commission has made it clear: waiting for 2027 to build your vulnerability response process is a major compliance risk. If a zero-day vulnerability in your firmware or cloud backend is exploited in September 2026, your 24-hour reporting clock starts immediately.

To prepare, engineering teams must verify that their internal Product Security Incident Response Teams are equipped to triage vulnerabilities and communicate directly with ENISA's API.
