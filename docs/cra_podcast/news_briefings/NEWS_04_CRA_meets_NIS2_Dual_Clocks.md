Today's story highlights a critical operational overlap that is causing confusion across corporate legal and security departments: the intersection of the Cyber Resilience Act and the NIS2 Directive.

Many organization CISOs ask whether reporting an incident under NIS2 satisfies their obligations under the CRA, or vice versa.

The European Commission has clarified the regulatory distinction:

NIS2 governs entity operational security—protecting critical infrastructure, energy grids, and essential service operators against operational disruption.

The CRA governs product security—ensuring that physical hardware devices, embedded firmware, and commercial software packages placed on the market meet mandatory cybersecurity requirements.

If an industrial gateway in a water treatment plant experiences a cyberattack, both regulations may trigger simultaneously. Under NIS2, the plant operator has 24 hours to report the operational disruption to their national competent authority. Under CRA Article 14, the gateway manufacturer has 24 hours to report the underlying exploited product vulnerability to ENISA and the national CSIRT.

To prevent duplicated efforts and conflicting reports, CISOs must establish a unified incident response triage framework that maps NIS2 operational impacts alongside CRA product vulnerability disclosures.
