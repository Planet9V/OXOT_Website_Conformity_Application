[JIM MCKENNEY]
Welcome back to The CRA Briefing. I'm Jim Mckenney with an essential operational update on Regulation [pronunciation: EU twenty-twenty-four slash twenty-eight-forty-seven].

The European Commission DG CONNECT has published official regulatory guidance clarifying the crucial boundary between routine maintenance patching and 'substantial modifications' under Article 21.

For industrial automation engineers and plant operators, this answers a major uncertainty: routine security patches designed solely to fix vulnerabilities or maintain the intended safety profile do not constitute substantial modifications. They do not trigger a requirement to re-issue the EU Declaration of Conformity.

However, modifications that introduce new wireless interfaces, expand the operational scope, or alter the underlying risk and threat profile legally reclassify the modifying entity—including third-party system integrators—as a 'Manufacturer' subject to full CE reassessment and 10-year technical file liabilities.

This formal guidance establishes clear safe-harbor rules for plant maintenance while establishing rigorous guardrails for system integrators.

Until next time: build secure by design, ship with confidence. I'm Jim Mckenney—thanks for listening.
