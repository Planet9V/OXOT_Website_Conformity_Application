Standard disclaimer: this news briefing provides technical commentary, not formal legal advice.

Today's focus is the operational reality of CRA Article 10, paragraph 6: the requirement for every product manufacturer to maintain formal vulnerability handling processes.

In recent industry briefings, regulatory authorities have clarified that compliance with Article 14's 24-hour reporting clock requires a dedicated Product Security Incident Response Team—or PSIRT.

Unlike traditional enterprise IT security teams that manage internal office networks, a PSIRT is responsible specifically for product security: triaging vulnerabilities in shipped hardware, firmware, and connected cloud backends.

Your PSIRT must be capable of executing three specific operational tasks:

First: Operating a public Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure portal with a security dot text file and PGP encryption key for external researchers.

Second: Triaging reported bugs using standardized CVSS version 4 scoring within hours of receipt.

Third: Automatically triggering ENISA notifications via the Single Reporting Platform within the mandatory 24-hour and 72-hour windows.

If your company currently routes security reports to a generic info or support email address, you are unprepared for September 11, 2026. Building your PSIRT workflow now is the single most critical step you can take to safeguard market access in the EU.
